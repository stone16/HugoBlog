## IMPACT ON ADRS

The ADRs in `docs/adr/` were read in full. Two contain conclusions damaged by the refuted assumptions; the rest need no factual correction from C1–C12. In particular, C1 confirms ADR-0005's warning about a dotted artifact prefix, and C5–C7 confirm ADR-0003's cookie/Origin threat model (with the implementation caveats recorded above).

### ADR-0001 — D1 is the sole source of truth for Posts

Wrong sentence:

> “Durability rests on Cloudflare: D1 Time Travel gives 30-day point-in-time restore, and `wrangler d1 export --remote` produces an on-demand SQL dump.”

This is wrong for a Free-plan deployment and its command is not executable as written because it omits the database and output path.

Corrected sentence:

> “Durability rests on Cloudflare: D1 Time Travel gives 7-day point-in-time recovery on Workers Free or 30 days on Workers Paid, and `npx wrangler d1 export <database_name> --remote --output=./database.sql` produces an on-demand SQL dump; schedule and test off-platform exports when retention beyond that window is required.”

This correction does not overturn D1 as the source of truth, but it removes a claimed 30-day Free recovery guarantee. A sole-source-of-truth system should not treat Time Travel as its only backup: export cadence, restore drills, retention, and storage outside the same failure/credential boundary need explicit operational ownership.

### ADR-0004 — First-party Comments in D1 replace Giscus

Wrong sentence:

> “Bot resistance comes from Cloudflare Turnstile, chosen over reCAPTCHA because it is reachable from mainland China.”

Cloudflare states the opposite: “Turnstile is not supported in Mainland China.” The selection rationale is therefore void, not merely weakened.

Corrected sentence:

> “Bot resistance uses a first-party layered control: rate limits and a honeypot on every submission, with a self-hosted ALTCHA proof-of-work challenge escalated for untrusted or suspicious clients; Turnstile is not used because Cloudflare documents it as unsupported in Mainland China.”

#### Replacement comparison for a Chinese-majority readership

| Option | Mainland reachability | Bot resistance | Reader friction | Judgment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-hosted ALTCHA proof-of-work | **High by architecture** when its script and challenge endpoint are served from `stometa.dev`; it has no required third-party challenge host. ALTCHA says it is “fully self-hosted.” Actual reachability still depends on the blog's own hosting path. | **Medium alone; medium-high when layered.** Basic proof-of-work prices out indiscriminate spam but sophisticated bots can solve or outsource it. ALTCHA recommends its additional Sentinel threat detection for stronger production protection. | **Low for most readers.** ALTCHA describes a “Frictionless experience for most users (no interaction required),” though proof-of-work costs battery/CPU and needs an accessible fallback. | **Recommended challenge layer**, combined with rate limiting, honeypot, first-comment moderation, replay protection, and adaptive difficulty. Sources: https://altcha.org/docs/ and https://altcha.org/docs/v2/proof-of-work-captcha |
| Self-hosted mCaptcha | **High by architecture** if all assets/endpoints are served first-party; no blocked third-party widget dependency. | **Medium.** Proof-of-work has the same economic-deterrence strength and limits as ALTCHA. | **Low-to-medium.** It avoids image puzzles, but adds client computation and another service to deploy and operate. | Viable, but less attractive here than ALTCHA because a separate self-hosted service increases operational surface for a Workers-centric personal blog without a demonstrated security advantage. Source: https://github.com/mCaptcha/mCaptcha |
| hCaptcha | **UNCERTAIN.** Its public integration requires `https://js.hcaptcha.com/1/api.js` and server verification at `https://api.hcaptcha.com/siteverify`; its primary documentation reviewed here makes no Mainland-China availability guarantee. | **High against commodity and interactive abuse**, with managed risk analysis and optional visual challenges. | **Medium-to-high.** A third-party widget can display an interactive visual challenge and introduces an external availability/privacy dependency. | Do not choose it on assumption alone. It requires real multi-region Mainland tests and an acceptable failover path before adoption. Source: https://docs.hcaptcha.com/ |
| Rate limiting + honeypot, no CAPTCHA | **High** because it is entirely first-party. | **Low-to-medium.** Strong against bursts and naive form-fillers; weak against distributed, browser-driven, or targeted bots. First-comment moderation prevents public spam but does not eliminate queue abuse. | **Lowest**: no visible or computational challenge for normal readers. | Use as the always-on baseline, not the only control. Escalate to proof-of-work based on trust/risk rather than challenging every commenter. |

The recommended design is therefore **rate limit + honeypot + moderation by default, adaptive self-hosted ALTCHA when risk rises**. The constraint ruling out Turnstile is Cloudflare's explicit Mainland unsupported status; the constraint ruling out hCaptcha as the default is the absence of a primary-source Mainland availability guarantee plus its mandatory third-party endpoints. This layered option preserves mainland reachability and low routine friction while making automated abuse more expensive; it must still include nonce expiry/single use, server-side proof verification, IP/privacy-aware throttles, and a no-JavaScript/accessibility fallback.

### Other design-level corrections not attached to an existing ADR sentence

- Budget **Workers Paid at US$5/account/month minimum** for reliable production SSR/authentication; validate actual CPU distributions under production-like traffic before setting the limit. Free is not a safe architectural promise for this workload.
- Configure both apex and wildcard Worker routing (`stometa.dev/*` plus `*.stometa.dev/*`, or a carefully tested equivalent); `*.stometa.dev/*` alone excludes the apex.
- Keep `__Host-` cookies and exact fail-closed Origin validation together. SameSite is not an origin boundary, and same-site attacker-controlled subdomains can initiate credentialed requests even when they cannot read CORS-protected responses.
